# Red Tech Priests of Mars — Construction and Defensive Architecture Doctrine

**Status:** Canonical visual and modeling style guide for Red Tech Priest constructions in the Tech-Priests project  
**Established:** 2026-11-05  
**Scope:** Original project structures, especially fortified machine emplacements, bunkers, automated defenses, installations, and later industrial buildings  
**Authority:** Project-owner art direction. This document governs visual design; it does **not** override `docs/STANDARDS_AND_PRACTICES.md`, recovery work orders, gameplay behavior, engine constraints, or release gates.  
**Intellectual-property boundary:** This is a project-specific, Warhammer 40,000 / Adeptus Mechanicus-inspired visual brief, not an assertion of official Games Workshop canon or authorization to reuse third-party models, art, symbols, or trademarks commercially.

> **Core dictum:** Brutalist industrial militarism fused with gothic mechanistic orthodoxy. The machine is sacred; human comfort is irrelevant. Every visible feature must reinforce, protect, operate, sense, transmit, maintain, identify, or consecrate the machinery.

## 1. Architectural identity

The Red Tech Priests of Mars are fanatical, purpose-driven industrialists. Their defensive works evoke late-World-War-II German reinforced-concrete fortification massing combined with uncompromising mechanical engineering and gothic machine worship.

The first read is **fortified, dense, rigid, heavy, inhumanly efficient**. A concrete bunker may be little more than several massive interlocked slabs with a mechanically traversing armored turret above them, yet its surfaces are alive with necessary control and sensing infrastructure: cables, conduits, optics, antennae, junction boxes, instrumentation, and prescribed machine-rites.

The resulting shape must communicate engineering purpose before ornament. It is neither a sleek spacecraft nor a fantasy cathedral with a gun attached.

### Non-negotiable axioms

1. **No superfluity.** Every feature must have a named structural, protective, mechanical, electrical, control, sensing, inspection, maintenance, identification, or ritual purpose.
2. **Brutalist mass.** Use ferrocrete blocks, poured slabs, cast shells, thick parapets, stepped profiles, exposed reinforcement, load-bearing buttresses and ribs.
3. **Mechanical legibility.** Weapons, bearings, traverse axes, recoil paths, ammo feeds or power conduits must form a comprehensible system.
4. **Functional gothic architecture.** Gothic ribs, niches, alcoves, crenulations and buttresses are structural, defensive, service-related, or sensory; they are never pasted-on fantasy trim.
5. **Machine awareness is mandatory.** The emplacement senses its environment, weapon state, and own machinery; remote detection and control must have credible hardware.
6. **Every signal travels somewhere.** Route sensors through visible protected power/data conduits into controllers, telemetry cabinets, antennae or local control mechanisms. Unconnected antennas and meaningless wires are design failures.
7. **Sanctification is installed equipment.** Rites of purity, consecration and machine veneration occupy prescribed inspection points, sealed covers, sensor niches, oiling stations or operating assemblies.
8. **No comfort-first ergonomics.** Humans and servitors are expendable to this culture. Safe, ergonomic access or hospitable working spaces are not design priorities, though service routes required for machine operation still exist.
9. **Armor and survival before silhouette tricks.** Weapon mounts, exposed joints, sensing heads and supply entries need rational protection; place vulnerable apparatus intentionally, not randomly.
10. **Readability at Factorio scale.** The bunker, weapon, sensor suite and machine-clergy identity must remain distinguishable when rendered small.

## 2. Functional hierarchy of parts

Each visible part must be classifiable. A part may occupy several roles at once.

| System | Preferred forms and details | Necessary physical connection or purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation / primary load path | Thick ferrocrete slabs, stepped bunker mass, bearing pedestals, buttresses, arch ribs | Carries turret and roof load into ground; resists blast and recoil |
| Armor / ballistic protection | Bolted plate cheeks, mantlets, low parapets, armored hatches, recessed slots | Protects weapon machinery, magazines, controllers and service penetrations |
| Weapons / actuation | Rotating ring, trunnions, recoil cylinders, elevation drives, loading path, mechanical locks | Clear traverse/elevation/recoil volumes and mount supports |
| Power / services | Armored cable ducts, feedthroughs, junction boxes, transformer or capacitor cabinets, vent pipes | Credible runs from source through control/power boxes to load |
| Sensing / targeting | Stereo optics, rangefinders, auspex arrays, thermal pickups, tracking heads, vibration and alignment gauges | Protected field of view and wired path to targeting/control hardware |
| Communications / remote control | Mast, short aerials, dish, hardline/vox transceiver, antenna foundations | Secure mounts, transmission path and control connection |
| Machine rites / authentication | Cog-shrine at brace, skull sensor enclosure, purity certificate on inspection cover, oiling port, incense/chemical sampling burner | Ritual purpose with a physical installation, interface, anchor or inspection function |
| Repair / service | Harsh ladders, narrow access hatches, lifting eyes, removable armor panels, bleed points | Reachable machinery without requiring comfort |
| Identification / status | Martian red armored panels, hazard marks, serial castings, machine purity plates, status lenses | Functional recognition, inspection and status communication |

**Functional redundancy:** Duplicate sensors, routes or armored pathways only where they provide deliberate fault tolerance or independent coverage. Never add multiple identical boxes just to make a wall look busy.

## 3. Forms and massing

- Primary volumes: low rectangular ferrocrete prisms, interlocked slabs, thick plinths, tapered or stepped defensive faces.
- Corners: hard, reinforced, often chamfered for protective purpose; avoid smooth consumer-product styling.
- Horizontal emphasis: broad anchored base and recessed openings, with enough vertical mass for supports and instruments.
- Upper profile: weapon housing and armored mount remain the focal mass; limited antennae, sensor heads and vent stacks break the skyline.
- Asymmetry: acceptable when caused by real magazine placement, equipment cabinets, access paths, exhaust or antenna fields.
- Openings: recessed and defensible. Narrow entries, service slots, armored shutters and protected cable penetrations rather than generous windows.
- Gothic ribs and pointed alcoves must be modeled as load-bearing ribs, shell stiffeners, braced service bays or blast-relief structure. If a niche contains a cog/skull icon, the framing still carries a legitimate reinforcing or access function.
- Material removed for a niche or recess must not silently compromise the main load path. Show wall thickness, buttressing and connection to the slab or bracing system.

### Historical reference language

Use the **visual grammar** of concrete WWII fortified structures: shuttered apertures, formwork seams, poured mass, thick embrasures, anti-blast offsets and service access. Do not copy historical insignia or propaganda. Gothic additions must remain inseparable from the engineering.

## 4. Weapons and protected machine operation

A defensive emplacement should show:

- Foundation supporting an actual traverse ring or weapon cradle.
- Gun breech, armored mantlet, recoil accommodation, elevation drive and exposed or implied bearing.
- Credible ammunition transfer or energy supply. A ballistic system needs an ammunition route; an energy weapon needs a power and thermal route.
- Mechanical and digital interfaces for rangefinding, targeting, firing and condition monitoring.
- Turret movement envelope clear of cable runs, rigid sensors, walls and access hatches. Use protected rotary couplings or deliberately routed flexible loops where rotation needs them.
- Some heat disposal, spent-material handling, drainage or maintenance access consistent with the weapon.
- Sensor packages positioned to observe useful arcs and protected from immediate muzzle blast or obstruction.

Avoid: unsupported floating guns; armor that blocks traverse; recoil mechanisms without travel; pipes to nowhere; magazines and conduits that inexplicably intersect the turret bearing.

## 5. Sensor and communications doctrine

Machine awareness is not optional decorative clutter. A representative fortified emplacement should include the kinds of equipment demanded by its mission:

- Local optical or thermal targeting head.
- Secondary range or tracking sensor with justified arc and line of sight.
- Status instrumentation on the weapon and actuators.
- Environmental or structural condition monitoring appropriate to exposure.
- Control/telemetry junctions and antenna or hardline network connection.
- Protected power/data trunk between instrument packages and controllers.

Sensor distribution should **express coverage**. A dorsal mast can carry long-range communication; a protected cheek mount can handle local targeting; a low inset package can watch approaches. Remote-control infrastructure must be visibly distinct from targeting optics.

Device labels, cable routes and status lamps should communicate actual subsystems. Model both sensor bodies and their supporting brackets / mounting plates.

## 6. Sacred machinery and gothic discipline

The visual culture venerates the Omnissiah and the machine spirit through ritualized engineering practice.

**Permitted functional devotional constructions:**

- A cog-skull motif integrated into a **structural rib, casting, armored sensor enclosure or bolted reinforcement plate**.
- A small sanctification recess at a **machinery inspection point**, with load-bearing niche frame.
- A parchment or stamped certificate of machine purity attached to a **specific serviced component**, access panel, serial casting or sealed controller.
- An oiling/blessing port connected to an actual bearing, gear train or service manifold.
- A ritual incense burner or vapor sensor positioned at a **real airflow, heat or environmental sampling point**.
- Gear-shaped bracing or vent grilles that really hold, guard, or ventilate something.
- Mechanistic script inscribed on **replaceable machine panels, inspection instructions or hazard boundaries**.

Religious elements must have real anchoring, meaningful scale and adjacency to the system they sanctify. Repetitive decorative skulls, hanging chains, enormous banners and random cathedral ornament are not this style.

## 7. Materials, color and surface history

**Primary:** stained poured ferrocrete / reinforced concrete, cast ferrous armor, bolted steel plate and machined metal.  
**Secondary:** aged brass on fittings or sacred instruments, heat-resistant ceramic, armored conduit and hardened optical glazing.  
**Paint:** deep Martian red for sanctioned armor, machine housings, unit identification and doctrinal markings; it should not erase the industrial material hierarchy.  
**Inserts:** bone/parchment for certificate or seal, dark oil, oxidized iron, soot, dust and restrained metallic wear.

Texture passes should make mechanical sense:

- Formwork lines and pour seams on ferrocrete.
- Scraped paint and chipped edges on armor exposed to service or fire.
- Grease and oil runoff near bearings, hatches and hydraulic points.
- Heat staining near barrels, vents and exhaust.
- Dirt catchment in lower creases and surface drains.
- Fasteners at actual plate joins and removable panels, not evenly scattered for decoration.

Avoid bright multicolor toy-like panels, plastic futuristic shells, soft civilian design, fantasy stone masonry and generalized random grunge.

## 8. Modular construction families

The same vocabulary must scale to the eventual library:

1. **Small protected sensor or remote-gun node:** short heavy plinth, armored weapon cheek, exposed sensor mast, local controller box.
2. **Standard fortified turret bunker:** ferrocrete ring/roof, bolted weapon housing, protected access, clear feed route and a broad sensing array.
3. **Heavy siege or artillery casemate:** massive slab buttresses, prominent recoil and magazine handling, protected long-distance instrumentation, armored service equipment.
4. **AA / point-defense installation:** unobstructed traverse, elevated sensor aperture, protected magazines and thermal systems.
5. **Command and monitoring bastion:** strong communication/sensing silhouette, hardened electronics, multiple protected antenna connections.
6. **Support and machine-service shelter:** compact maintenance bays, charging/oil lines, spare component handling and dedicated consecration points.

A family should share recognizable formwork, mounting grids, fastener vocabulary, field-repair plates, sensor mounts, material assignments and Martian red markings without every asset being identical.

## 9. Modeling and review workflow

### Visible-geometry budget (mandatory)

**If a feature cannot be seen in the intended game-camera render, do not model it.** The final in-game image at Factorio scale, not a hypothetical physical cutaway, defines the geometry budget. Describe hidden mechanisms in documentation rather than constructing invisible meshes, whether plausible or not. Model real depth and surfaces where they change visible shading, silhouettes, cast shadows, openings or component overlaps; omit interiors, unseen undersides and unrequested hidden machinery. No underside inspection model merely to prove that an imagined device could operate.

For the emergency miner, the owner-authoritative principle is **face-down modulated laser ablation and very slow, polluting proto-matter reconstitution**, but the sprite shows an armored **upper control interface**. Reconstruct that *visible box*, with no auger and no invisible laser/electronics machinery.


**Goal:** High-detail original 3D objects recognizable as their intended 2D sprite subject and renderable as polished Factorio-scale images. Work on **one asset at a time**.

1. **Reference contract.** Identify the specific existing sprite or approved design brief, camera orientation, reference dimensions, intended game footprint, weapon mission and obvious mechanical constraints.
2. **Structural primitive blockout.** Build only the ferrocrete foundation, load-bearing ribs/buttresses, turret mounting volume and principal weapon mass. Check proportions and silhouette from the target isometric camera.
3. **Mechanical proof.** Add traverse and elevation axes, armor interfaces, recoil path, ammo/power path, thermal services and basic maintenance access. Review for impossible intersections or unsupported mass.
4. **Sensor and service network.** Place real optics, rangefinders, antennae, control cabinets, brackets, feedthroughs and routed cables. Every element needs a start, end and named function.
5. **Functional gothic pass.** Integrate a limited number of sacred icons, certificate/inspection markers and ritual equipment into structural and service systems.
6. **Detail refinement.** Model seams, bolted plates, casting lines, access plates, vents, fasteners, protectors, wear and materials at the level required to match the visual finish of native Factorio objects.
7. **Render validation.** Use fixed camera, lighting, exposure and target game scale. Compare reference and model side by side; inspect silhouette, visual hierarchy, legibility, proportions, texture noise and shadowing.
8. **Accept/revise.** Do not advance merely because the high-poly mesh contains many parts. Reject incorrect silhouettes, impossible mechanics, unconnected systems, gratuitous ornament and low-resolution mush.

**Review principle:** Refinement must improve the original object recognition, not turn it into a generic "sci-fi turret." An original source sprite may be refined, but departures must be intentional and documented.

## 10. Per-asset acceptance checklist

- [ ] The object is unmistakably the agreed structure or sprite at game scale.
- [ ] Heavy ferrocrete mass and armor clearly establish an industrial fortification.
- [ ] Turret and weapon mechanics have a plausible physical support and movement envelope.
- [ ] Armor protects meaningful volumes without contradicting operation.
- [ ] Sensor types, locations, fields of view and connections are purposeful.
- [ ] Antennae have credible mounts and corresponding power/control infrastructure.
- [ ] Every major wire, hose and conduit has an identifiable origin and endpoint.
- [ ] Gothic arches and alcoves are actual ribs, braces, access constructs or armor features.
- [ ] Gear/skull and sanctification equipment are integrated, not glued-on clutter.
- [ ] Materials and wear correspond to actual use, age and environment.
- [ ] Maintenance routes exist without pretending the design values human comfort.
- [ ] Final isometric render achieves native Factorio-like readable quality; otherwise the asset is not accepted.
- [ ] Rights and provenance of any imported resources are documented; original project geometry is preferred.

## 11. Canonical first exemplar: Fortified Martian Heavy-Weapon Emplacement

**Construction concept:** Several thick, interlocked ferrocrete slabs form a reinforced low casemate. Recessed gothic arches stiffen the supporting foundation. A compact, armored traversing heavy-weapon mount sits on an anchored ring above. The weapon has a believable breech and recoil or energy feed, bolted mantlet plates, maintenance hatches and protected supply conduits. Near it: local optical/thermal sensor packages, a ranging device, an antenna mast, controllers and diagnostic junctions. Their cabling is physically routed. A structurally integrated cog-and-skull dedication and one or two machine-purity service markers finish the devotional layer.

The first modeling deliverable is **accurate structure and mechanical silhouette**, not an ornamented final render. Once geometry is proven, add the sensor network, sanctification, material detail and game-scale rendering.

## 12. Explicit exclusions

This is **not**: a delicate fantasy shrine; ornate gothic palace; symmetrical prop clutter for its own sake; sleek or comfortable civilian sci-fi; generic polygon turret on a cube; random skulls and dangling wires without functions; a claim that a high polygon count alone proves quality; or authorization to use paid/restricted 40K meshes in the mod.

---

**Short-form doctrine for future briefs:**  
**Red Tech Priest architecture is brutalist fortified machine worship: ferrocrete, armor, mechanistic gothic load paths, dense functional sensors, physically routed conduits, ritualized machine maintenance, and no design concession to human comfort. Every visible feature has a purpose; every purpose is made legible.**
